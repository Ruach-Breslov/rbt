# Ruach Breslov website strategy

Status: local working strategy, September 2026. This document guides the current redesign; it does not authorize publication of unconfirmed events, testimonials, schedules, or program claims.

## What the organization is here to do

Ruach Breslov presents itself as a growing community that brings Rebbe Nachman's teachings into real life through Torah, emunah, joy, honesty, and connection. Its own material emphasizes a brotherhood rather than only a class: weekly learning, one-to-one mentorship, food and music, guest teachers, outreach, and a welcome for people who feel distant, uncertain, or in need of light.

This aligns with several recurring Breslov themes described by the Breslov Research Institute: practical and achievable personal growth; faith, joy, sincerity, and simplicity in everyday life; finding the good in oneself and others; and hitbodedut as personal prayer in one's own words. The site should express those themes through warmth and clarity, not through decorative mysticism or vague wellness language.

## People the site needs to serve

1. A newcomer asking, “Will I fit in?” They need to see real people, a judgment-free welcome, plain language, a location, and an easy way to speak with someone.
2. A community member asking, “What is happening next?” They need confirmed dates, current announcements, recordings, and simple sign-up flows.
3. A person looking for Torah that speaks to real life. They need a searchable, well-organized video library and clear paths into classes or mentorship.
4. A supporter asking, “What will my gift make possible?” They need concrete impact, secure one-time and recurring options, and transparent contact/privacy information.
5. A service verifying the organization. It needs a credible public identity, working HTTPS, consistent contact details, active content, privacy disclosure, and clear programs.

## Experience principles

- Human before institutional: use real rooms, faces, tables, books, and moments of welcome.
- Simple without being sparse: Breslov simplicity means sincerity and directness, not an empty beige interface.
- Joy with gravity: use warmth, movement, music, and shared life without turning the brand into confetti or hype.
- Torah in real life: every abstract idea should lead to a class, conversation, story, video, or next step.
- Come as you are: avoid insider-heavy language where a plain explanation can invite someone in.
- Proof over promises: publish only confirmed events, real photography, official recordings, and approved stories.

## Visual direction

The client-selected sunset artwork is the welcome moment. It supplies gold, firelight, stone, black, and parchment; the supporting system adds oxblood and quiet sage for contrast. Dark cinematic sections hold spiritual weight, while warm paper surfaces keep reading personal. Typography should feel editorial and established, with clean sans-serif controls for practical tasks.

The design should avoid generic charity cards, excessive rounded pills, synthetic gradients, staged stock photography, and repeated marketing slogans. Real community photography and the cinematic gallery should carry the emotional proof.

## Content architecture

- Home: unmistakable identity, candid invitation, real community proof, offerings, origin story, and one simple stay-connected action.
- Events: only confirmed gatherings, with date, time, address, audience, accessibility, and RSVP expectations.
- Gallery: the cinematic community view; captions should identify the kind of moment without inventing names or stories.
- Videos: channel-style Videos/Shorts tabs, local thumbnails, sorting, search, and a click-to-load privacy-enhanced player.
- Contact: clear person-to-person path, public details, expectations for replies, visiting information, and practical FAQs.
- Support: one-time and monthly giving with concrete, approved impact language and self-service billing access.

## Recommended next phases

### Phase 1 — Rescue facelift (current)

- Establish the richer visual system and client-selected hero.
- Make the homepage sound like people speaking, not a campaign template.
- Turn the official channel into a useful video library with a closable animated player.
- Preserve multilingual, accessibility, privacy, and static-hosting behavior.

### Phase 2 — Add the people

- Collect two or three approved participant stories in their own words.
- Add a short introduction from the person who welcomes newcomers.
- Confirm whether teachers and mentors may be named and photographed.
- Replace repeated class titles such as “March 9, 2026” on YouTube with descriptive official titles where the channel owner approves changes.

### Phase 3 — Make attendance effortless

- Confirm weekly schedule, intended audience, access/parking, accessibility, and what a first-time visitor should expect.
- Publish real upcoming events and connect RSVP confirmations.
- Use a dedicated public “Ruach Breslov Events” Google Calendar as the recommended nontechnical editing source. Invite approved staff as calendar editors instead of sharing a password; they can create, revise, repeat, or cancel events in the familiar Calendar app.
- Sync that calendar into the site's own branded event cards so updates, cancellations, localized date formatting, map directions, and RSVP links remain clear without asking staff to edit code.
- Keep the organization's personal/internal calendars separate; only details placed on the dedicated public events calendar should be published.

### Phase 4 — Build a durable teaching library

- Group videos by teacher, sefer/Torah, topic, and format.
- Establish a light publishing routine so new official uploads reach the site promptly.
- Add short written takeaways only when reviewed by the teaching team.

### Phase 5 — Measure what helps

- Track privacy-conscious outcomes such as contact completions, event interest, video starts, and donation checkout starts.
- Review search terms and unanswered contact questions to improve navigation and content.
- Keep a quarterly content accuracy review for events, contact details, privacy language, and donation impact.
